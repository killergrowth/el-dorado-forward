Why this matters for El Dorado
We don’t need a ban

El Dorado already has rules for this

In June 2026 the City adopted zoning rules for data centers and battery storage. Every project needs a Special Use Permit: a public hearing, full disclosure of water and power needs, and the City’s right to set conditions or say no.

How the City’s rules work
El Dorado Lake

Water is not a problem

El Dorado has one of the biggest water advantages in Kansas. Our lake holds over 50 billion gallons, and the City has 10 million gallons a day set aside for industry.

The headlines about data centers using millions of gallons a day describe evaporative cooling, where water is used up as it cools the building. Many new data centers use closed-loop systems instead. The loop is filled once during construction and the same water circulates for years. Microsoft says its new closed-loop sites use about as much water in a year as a single restaurant.

Property taxes

Growth can lower your tax bill

A large new project spreads the cost of local government across more value. If local governments hold their revenue flat, mill levies fall for everyone else. And Kansas’s data center law is a sales tax break, not a property tax break.

What’s on the ballot

Four lines in the ordinance, four problems

10 years

Locked in for a decade

Once passed, the City Commission can’t adjust it for ten years. The only fix is another election. A decade is a long time to hang a “closed” sign on El Dorado while other towns compete for the same investment.

City of El Dorado · Jul 14, 2026
10 MW

The line is drawn very low

Florida’s incentive law defines a data center at 100 megawatts. This ban starts at one-tenth of that, so it reaches far beyond giant AI campuses to smaller server and computing facilities.

NCSL · Apr 1, 2026
100,000

Gallons a day is a tiny slice

That’s 1% of the 10 million gallons a day the City has set aside for industry. The ban applies no matter how efficient the cooling design, and the City already requires water numbers before any approval.

City of El Dorado Ordinance G-1463 · Jun 2026
80 kWh

It bans batteries, too

Battery storage above 80 kWh is prohibited, not just at data centers. That covers systems businesses and utilities use for backup power and grid reliability, which national fire codes and the City’s permit process already regulate.

City of El Dorado · Aug 21, 2026 NFPA 855
