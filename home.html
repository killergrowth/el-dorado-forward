Why this matters for El Dorado
We don't need a ban

El Dorado already has rules for this

In June 2026 the City adopted zoning rules for data centers and battery storage. Every project needs a Special Use Permit: a public hearing, full disclosure of water and power needs, and the City's right to set conditions or say no.

Source City Commission Summary / Legal Brief on the Petition Ordinance · City Manager, City of El Dorado · Jul 13, 2026 Also: Zoning Amendments for Emerging Industries (Ordinance G-1463)

How the City's rules work
What the Special Use Permit requires
  • ✓ Public hearing before any approval
  • ✓ Full disclosure of water usage
  • ✓ Full disclosure of power requirements
  • ✓ City right to set binding conditions
  • ✓ City right to deny any application
  • ✓ Ongoing compliance monitoring

These rules are already in place under Ordinance G-1463, adopted June 2026. The ballot question would override them with a blanket ban.

Water advantage

El Dorado has water most cities don't

El Dorado Reservoir holds over 200,000 acre-feet — one of the largest water reserves in Kansas. Modern data centers increasingly use closed-loop cooling that recycles water rather than consuming it.

The refinery already uses far more water than any data center would.

Property taxes

Growth can lower your tax bill

A large new project spreads the cost of local government across more value. If local governments hold their revenue flat, mill levies fall for everyone else. And Kansas's data center law is a sales tax break, not a property tax break.

What's on the ballot

Four lines in the ordinance, four problems

Source Citizen-Initiated Petition Ballot Question · City of El Dorado · Aug 31, 2026 Also: Full ballot analysis

10 yr

A decade-long ban

The ordinance prohibits data centers and battery storage for 10 full years — a generation of economic development, frozen by a single vote.

2 types

Two industries banned outright

Data centers and battery energy storage are both prohibited — regardless of size, location, water use, or the City's negotiated conditions.

1 vote

Only a public vote can reverse it

If circumstances change — new technology, a compelling project, a community that changes its mind — the City Commission can't act. Another election is required.

$0

Zero exceptions, zero flexibility

No carve-outs for small projects, renewable-powered facilities, or already-approved investments. The ban is absolute.
