Water & infrastructure

Water isn’t the problem

El Dorado has water to spare.

The lake holds over 50 billion gallons, and the City has 10 million gallons a day set aside for industry. We still owe $39.8 million to the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers for that storage, and industrial customers help pay it.

El Dorado City FAQIndustrial Water AllocationLake Debt Documentation

Closed-loop cooling uses very little water once filled.

The headlines about data centers using millions of gallons a day describe evaporative cooling, where water is used up as it cools the building. Many new data centers use closed-loop systems instead. The loop is filled once during construction and the same water circulates for years. Microsoft says its new closed-loop sites use about as much water in a year as a single restaurant. After the initial fill, the main ongoing use is restrooms and kitchens.

Microsoft Zero Water CoolingNadella: Data Center Water UseVantage Closed-Loop Cooling

There is no single “data centers use X gallons” number.

Berkeley Lab found very large variation by server efficiency, cooling technology, grid characteristics, and climate.

The Water Use of Data Center Workloads

The ordinance draws its line at 100,000 gallons a day.

That is 1% of the City’s industrial water. A facility above that daily draw is “high impact” and banned, whatever its cooling design.

Ordinance G-1463 (Ballot Text)Industrial Water Allocation

G-1463 requires water numbers up front.

Average-day and peak-day demand, process water, fire flow, cooling type, and wastewater discharge must be described before approval.

Power & utilities

Power is reviewed up front

10 MW is the ordinance’s power trigger.

Any computing facility with a peak electrical load above ten megawatts is “high impact.”

Ordinance G-1463 (Ballot Text)

Kansas ties its incentive to a 10-year power contract.

To qualify for the state incentive, a data center must enter a 10-year electricity purchase agreement with its local utility.

Subsidizing Servers: How States Are Competing to Attract Data CentersK.S.A. 79-3606 (Kansas data center sales tax exemption)

Applicants must show their utility path.

G-1463 requires peak load in megawatts, planned interconnections, backup generation inventory, and Evergy Path to Power status.

Jobs, investment & taxes

Taxes could go down

Kansas created a data center incentive in July 2025.

Qualifying projects must invest at least $250 million within five years and create 20 new jobs within two years. The state can claw back benefits.

Subsidizing Servers: How States Are Competing to Attract Data CentersK.S.A. 79-3606 (Kansas data center sales tax exemption)

The Kansas exemption is a sales tax exemption.

NCSL reports it covers land, buildings, site improvements, and equipment, but specifically excludes electricity. It lists no state property tax abatement.

Subsidizing Servers: How States Are Competing to Attract Data Centers

A large project could lower homeowners’ property taxes.

In an illustrative $1 billion example with $300 million of taxable real property, revenue-neutral mill levies would save about $404 a year per $100,000 of home value, or about $1,010 on a $250,000 home.

Barber County’s Google data center gets no property tax abatement.

Google agreed to pay 100% of its property taxes plus $5.5 million in community benefit payments.

KAKE: Barber County Google Deal

Construction jobs are large. Permanent jobs are fewer.

NCSL notes construction can support many workers, while operating facilities create far fewer permanent positions.

Land use & community

Neighbors are protected

Setbacks are already required.

G-1463 requires a 500-foot setback between generator and cooling yards, substations, or fuel storage and residential districts or sensitive uses.

Legal Memo on G-1463

Noise can be conditioned.

The City may require acoustic enclosures, post-construction sound testing, and limits on generator testing.
